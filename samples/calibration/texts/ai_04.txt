The lighthouse had been dark for eleven years when Marguerite finally climbed its spiral staircase. Each step groaned beneath her boots, echoing through the hollow tower like a distant memory. Salt had crept into every crevice of the stone, and the air tasted of rust and old rain.

At the top, she paused to catch her breath and pressed her palm against the cold glass of the lantern room. Beyond it, the sea stretched out in endless shades of gray, restless and indifferent. Somewhere out there, her grandfather had once guided ships safely home through storms that had swallowed lesser men.

She knelt beside the dusty control panel and brushed away the cobwebs. Tucked beneath it, wrapped in oilcloth, was a small leather journal she had never seen before. Her heart quickened as she unwrapped it with trembling fingers. The first page held only a single line, written in her grandfather's careful hand: "If you are reading this, the light must be lit again."

Outside, a gull cried, and the wind began to rise.
